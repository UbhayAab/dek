## What the numbers mean

- The database is not the limit at these sizes: 6 active connections at 300 users.
  Realtime is the binding meter (500 peak connections included in Pro, see
  docs/COSTS.md; the project allows 10,000 so a spike is billed, not refused).
- Sign-in from one network is the only place people waited. After the first run the
  token limit was raised from 2,000 to 4,500 per 5 minutes per IP (15 a second after
  the burst of 30), which cut the 429s from 47 to 32 but cannot raise the burst. The
  client now retries a 429 on password sign-in quietly (up to 3 times, jittered), and
  a 429 on a token refresh is reported to auth-js as a retryable 503 so nobody is
  signed out by a rate limit (apps/web/src/api/client.ts).
- One NAT of 60 people signing in within the same second is the worst case measured
  here. When the same 300 sign-ins were spread over two minutes from that one IP (the
  sustained run's ramp), sign-in p95 was 0.28 s and nobody saw a 429.
